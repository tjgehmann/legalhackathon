# Regulatory Summaries — Plain Language Guide

This document provides plain-language summaries of key regulations that commonly apply to TechForward's projects and initiatives. These summaries are starting points — for detailed guidance, consult the full regulatory text or the Legal team.

---

## 1. GDPR — General Data Protection Regulation (EU)

**What it is:** The EU's comprehensive data protection law. It applies to any organization that processes personal data of individuals in the EU/EEA, regardless of where the organization is based.

**Who it applies to:** Any TechForward project that collects, stores, uses, or shares personal data of EU/EEA residents (customers, prospects, employees, website visitors).

**Key requirements:**
- **Lawful basis:** You need a valid legal reason to process personal data (consent, contract, legitimate interest, etc.)
- **Transparency:** Tell people what data you collect, why, and what their rights are (privacy notice)
- **Data minimization:** Only collect data you actually need
- **Purpose limitation:** Only use data for the purpose it was collected for
- **Data subject rights:** People can access, correct, delete, and port their data
- **Data protection by design:** Build privacy protections into your product from the start
- **Breach notification:** Report breaches to authorities within 72 hours
- **DPA required:** Any vendor processing personal data on your behalf needs a signed Data Processing Agreement

**Penalties:** Up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher.

## 2. CCPA/CPRA — California Consumer Privacy Act / California Privacy Rights Act

**What it is:** California's consumer privacy law, giving California residents specific rights over their personal information. CPRA amended and expanded CCPA effective January 2023.

**Who it applies to:** Businesses that collect personal information from California residents and meet certain thresholds (annual revenue > $25M, OR process data of 100,000+ California residents, OR derive 50%+ of revenue from selling/sharing personal data).

**Key requirements:**
- **Right to know:** Consumers can ask what personal information you collect and how it's used
- **Right to delete:** Consumers can request deletion of their personal information
- **Right to opt-out:** Consumers can opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information
- **Right to correct:** Consumers can request correction of inaccurate information
- **Non-discrimination:** You can't discriminate against consumers who exercise their rights
- **"Do Not Sell or Share" link:** Required on your website
- **Privacy policy:** Must include specific CCPA disclosures

**Penalties:** Up to $7,500 per intentional violation, $2,500 per unintentional violation.

## 3. CAN-SPAM Act (US)

**What it is:** The US federal law governing commercial email messages.

**Who it applies to:** Any TechForward email that promotes a commercial product or service to US recipients.

**Key requirements:**
- **No deceptive subject lines:** Subject lines must accurately reflect email content
- **Identify as advertisement:** The email must clearly be identified as an ad
- **Physical address:** Include your valid physical postal address
- **Unsubscribe mechanism:** Every email must include a clear way to opt out
- **Honor opt-outs promptly:** Process unsubscribe requests within 10 business days
- **Monitor third-party senders:** If someone sends email on your behalf, you're still responsible

**Key difference from GDPR:** CAN-SPAM allows opt-out (you can email until they unsubscribe). GDPR requires opt-in (you need consent before emailing). When emailing EU contacts, GDPR's stricter standard applies.

**Penalties:** Up to $51,744 per email in violation.

## 4. EU AI Act

**What it is:** The EU's comprehensive regulation on artificial intelligence, establishing a risk-based framework for AI systems.

**Who it applies to:** Any organization that develops, deploys, or uses AI systems in the EU market.

**Risk categories:**
- **Prohibited AI:** Manipulation, social scoring, real-time biometric identification (with exceptions) — these are banned
- **High-risk AI:** AI in critical areas (employment decisions, creditworthiness, law enforcement, etc.) — subject to strict requirements
- **Limited-risk AI:** Chatbots, deepfakes, emotion recognition — must meet transparency obligations (tell users they're interacting with AI)
- **Minimal-risk AI:** Most AI applications — voluntary codes of conduct

**Key requirements for high-risk systems:**
- Risk management system
- Data governance and quality
- Technical documentation
- Record-keeping and logging
- Transparency and user information
- Human oversight mechanisms
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity

**Penalties:** Up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global annual turnover for the most serious violations.

## 5. AML/KYC — Anti-Money Laundering / Know Your Customer

**What it is:** A set of regulations requiring businesses (especially in financial services) to verify the identity of their customers and monitor transactions for suspicious activity.

**Who it applies to:** TechForward when engaging with financial services customers, processing financial data, or operating in regulated markets.

**Key requirements:**
- **Customer identification:** Verify the identity of customers and beneficial owners
- **Customer due diligence:** Assess the risk level of each customer
- **Sanctions screening:** Check customers against EU, US, UK, and UN sanctions lists
- **Transaction monitoring:** Monitor for unusual or suspicious patterns
- **Suspicious activity reporting:** Report suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit
- **Record-keeping:** Maintain records for at least 5 years

**Key for TechForward:** Even if we're not a financial institution ourselves, when we partner with financial services companies or process financial data, these requirements may flow down to us through contractual obligations.

## 6. German Labor Law (Key Points for HR Projects)

**What it is:** Germany has among the strongest employee protection laws in the world. Key legislation includes the German Civil Code (BGB), Works Constitution Act (BetrVG), and various employment protection statutes.

**Who it applies to:** Any TechForward project affecting employees in Germany (our primary workforce).

**Key points:**
- **Works council rights:** The works council (Betriebsrat) must be consulted before introducing new systems that monitor employee behavior or performance. Failure to consult can make the system illegal to use.
- **Employee data protection:** Employee data receives extra protection under German law. Processing must be necessary for the employment relationship.
- **Unfair dismissal protection:** After 6 months of employment, employees are protected against unfair dismissal. Termination requires valid grounds and proper process.
- **Working time:** Maximum working hours, rest periods, and holiday entitlements are strictly regulated.
- **Equal treatment:** Strong anti-discrimination protections based on race, gender, religion, disability, age, and sexual orientation.

**Key for projects:** Any new tool or system that processes employee data or affects working conditions likely requires works council consultation. Plan for this early — it can add weeks to your timeline.

## 7. ePrivacy Directive (EU) — Cookie Rules

**What it is:** The EU directive governing electronic communications, including cookie usage and direct marketing. Often called the "Cookie Directive."

**Who it applies to:** Any TechForward website or application that uses cookies or similar tracking technologies for EU visitors.

**Key requirements:**
- **Consent before cookies:** Non-essential cookies require the user's consent before being placed
- **Informed consent:** Users must be told what cookies are used, their purpose, and duration
- **Granular choice:** Users should be able to accept some cookie categories and reject others
- **Easy withdrawal:** Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it
- **Strictly necessary exception:** Cookies essential for the website to function don't need consent (but do need disclosure)

**Practical impact:** Your website needs a cookie banner that blocks all non-essential cookies until the user makes a choice. Pre-checked boxes or "by continuing to browse you agree" banners don't meet the standard.
