# BGB AGB-Recht Reference — §§ 305–310 BGB

This document provides a structured reference of the key provisions governing Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen (AGB) under German law. Use this as the legal framework for assessing whether AGB clauses are permissible.

---

## § 305 BGB — Einbeziehung Allgemeiner Geschäftsbedingungen (Incorporation of Standard Terms)

### Statutory Rule
AGB are all contract terms pre-formulated for a multitude of contracts that one party (the "user") presents to the other party upon conclusion of the contract. AGB become part of the contract only if:
1. The user expressly refers to them at the time of contract conclusion
2. The other party has the opportunity to take notice of their content in a reasonable manner
3. The other party agrees to their applicability

### Key Points for Review
- Terms that are so unusual that the other party need not expect them do **not** become part of the contract (§ 305c Abs. 1 BGB — "Überraschende Klauseln")
- Ambiguities in AGB are interpreted against the user (§ 305c Abs. 2 BGB — "Unklarheitenregel")

---

## § 307 BGB — Inhaltskontrolle (General Reasonableness Test)

### Statutory Rule
Provisions in AGB are void if they unreasonably disadvantage the other party contrary to the requirements of good faith. An unreasonable disadvantage is presumed if a provision:
1. Is incompatible with essential basic principles of the statutory provision from which it deviates (§ 307 Abs. 2 Nr. 1)
2. Restricts essential rights or obligations arising from the nature of the contract to such an extent that attainment of the contract's purpose is jeopardized (§ 307 Abs. 2 Nr. 2)

### Key Points for Review
- This is the **general catch-all** provision — applies whenever §§ 308, 309 do not specifically address the clause
- The test is whether the clause deviates from the **gesetzliches Leitbild** (statutory model) and whether this deviation is justified
- In B2B contracts, § 307 is the primary tool for AGB review (since §§ 308, 309 apply only indirectly via § 310 Abs. 1)

## § 308 BGB — Klauselverbote mit Wertungsmöglichkeit (Prohibited Clauses with Discretionary Assessment)

### Key Prohibitions

| Nr. | Prohibition | Statutory Default | Typical Problematic Clause |
|-----|-------------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Nr. 1** | Unreasonably long acceptance/performance deadlines | Performance due immediately or within reasonable time | "Delivery within 6 months of order" without justification |
| **Nr. 2** | Unreasonably long additional performance deadline | Reasonable deadline for cure | "Seller has 6 months to remedy any defect" |
| **Nr. 3** | Right of withdrawal reserved to user without objective reason | No unilateral withdrawal right | "We may cancel any order at our sole discretion" |
| **Nr. 4** | Right to modify performance reserved to user | Performance as agreed | "We may change the scope of services at any time" |
| **Nr. 5** | Deemed declarations (Fingierte Erklärungen) | Express declarations required | "Silence after 14 days constitutes acceptance" |
| **Nr. 7** | Unreasonable form requirements for notices | No specific form required unless by statute | "All notices must be sent by registered mail to three addresses" |
| **Nr. 8** | Unreasonably short limitation periods for defects (< 1 year for new goods) | 2 years for movable goods, 5 years for buildings | "All warranty claims must be raised within 3 months" |

## § 309 BGB — Klauselverbote ohne Wertungsmöglichkeit (Strictly Prohibited Clauses)

These clauses are **always void** in B2C contracts. In B2B contracts, they serve as strong indicators of unreasonableness under § 307.

### Key Prohibitions

| Nr. | Prohibition | What It Means | Example of Void Clause |
|-----|-------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Nr. 2** | Price increase clauses (short-term delivery) | Cannot increase price within 4 months of contract conclusion | "Prices may be adjusted to reflect cost increases at any time" |
| **Nr. 5** | Liquidated damages (Pauschalierter Schadensersatz) | Excessive flat-rate damages that exceed typical damage | "Late payment incurs a flat penalty of 25% of contract value" |
| **Nr. 7a** | Exclusion of liability for injury to life, body, or health due to negligence | Liability for personal injury can **never** be excluded | "Provider shall not be liable for any damages whatsoever" |
| **Nr. 7b** | Exclusion of liability for gross negligence | Liability for gross negligence can **never** be excluded in AGB | "Liability is limited to cases of intentional misconduct only" |
| **Nr. 8a** | Exclusion of right to withdraw due to defects (Rücktritt) | Buyer must retain right to withdraw if defect is not cured | "Buyer's sole remedy is repair; no right of withdrawal" |
| **Nr. 8b** | Limitation of defect claims to repair only | Cannot exclude replacement or price reduction | "All defect claims are limited exclusively to repair" |
| **Nr. 9** | Exclusion of liability for breach of essential contractual obligations (Kardinalpflichten) | Cannot exclude liability for the core obligations that make the contract meaningful | "Provider is not liable for failure to deliver the contracted services" |
| **Nr. 10** | Assignment of obligations to third parties without right of withdrawal | Cannot unilaterally substitute a different service provider | "We may assign all obligations to an affiliated company" |
| **Nr. 12** | Shifting the burden of proof to the disadvantage of the other party | Cannot impose unfair evidentiary obligations | "Customer must prove that the defect existed at the time of delivery" (in consumer sales) |
| **Nr. 13** | Form requirements for notices/declarations stricter than statutory form | Cannot require stricter form than the law requires | "Termination is only valid if notarized" |

## § 310 BGB — Anwendungsbereich (Scope of Application)

### B2B vs. B2C

| Provision | B2C Contracts | B2B Contracts (§ 310 Abs. 1) |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| § 305 (Incorporation) | Fully applies | Fully applies |
| § 305c (Surprising clauses) | Fully applies | Fully applies |
| § 307 (General reasonableness) | Fully applies | **Fully applies** — this is the main review standard for B2B |
| § 308 (Prohibited with discretion) | Directly applies | Applies **indirectly** — clauses are assessed under § 307 with § 308 as an indicator |
| § 309 (Strictly prohibited) | Directly applies (always void) | Applies **indirectly** — clauses are assessed under § 307 with § 309 as a strong indicator, but exceptions are possible in commercial practice |

### Key Principle for B2B Review
In B2B contracts, the prohibitions of §§ 308–309 are not automatically applicable but serve as **Indizwirkung** (indicative effect). A clause that would be void under § 309 in a consumer context is *likely* also void in B2B under § 307, but courts may allow deviations if they reflect reasonable commercial practice.

## Common AGB Issues by Clause Type

### Limitation of Liability (Haftungsbeschränkung)
- **Statutory default:** Full liability for all forms of fault (Vorsatz, grobe Fahrlässigkeit, einfache Fahrlässigkeit)
- **Permissible in AGB:** Limiting liability for simple negligence to foreseeable, contract-typical damages (vorhersehbare, vertragstypische Schäden)
- **Not permissible in AGB:** Excluding or limiting liability for intentional misconduct, gross negligence, or injury to life/body/health

### Warranty / Defects (Mängelhaftung)
- **Statutory default:** 2-year warranty period for movable goods (§ 438 BGB); buyer's choice of repair or replacement (§ 439 BGB)
- **Permissible in AGB:** Reasonable shortening of warranty period (minimum 1 year for new goods in B2B)
- **Not permissible in AGB:** Complete exclusion of warranty; limitation to repair only without right to replacement, price reduction, or withdrawal

### Termination (Kündigung)
- **Statutory default:** Varies by contract type; extraordinary termination for good cause (§ 314 BGB) can never be excluded
- **Permissible in AGB:** Reasonable notice periods; specification of termination procedures
- **Not permissible in AGB:** Excluding the right to extraordinary termination; excessively long minimum contract terms without justification

### Payment Terms (Zahlungsbedingungen)
- **Statutory default:** Payment due upon performance (§ 271 BGB); default interest at base rate + 5% for consumers, + 9% for B2B (§ 288 BGB)
- **Permissible in AGB:** Reasonable payment deadlines; contractual interest rates within bounds
- **Not permissible in AGB:** Excessive late payment penalties; unreasonable prepayment obligations without security

### Indemnification (Freistellung)
- **Statutory default:** No general indemnification obligation; each party is liable for its own breaches
- **Permissible in AGB:** Indemnification for third-party claims arising from the other party's breach
- **Not permissible in AGB:** Broad, unlimited indemnification obligations that shift the user's own liability risk to the other party
