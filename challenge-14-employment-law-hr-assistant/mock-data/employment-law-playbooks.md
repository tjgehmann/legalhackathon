# TechForward Employment Law Playbooks for HR

> Step-by-step routines for the most common employment law scenarios. These playbooks assume German law applies. Always check for applicable collective agreements (Tarifverträge) that may grant greater employee protections.

---

## HOW TO USE THESE PLAYBOOKS

1. **Identify the scenario** using the HR Scenario Catalog
2. **Run the protection check** (page 2) before proceeding with any dismissal scenario
3. **Follow the steps in order** — do not skip ahead
4. **Stop at any Legal escalation gate** — shown as ⛔ LEGAL GATE
5. **Use the templates** at the end of each playbook for all written communications

---

## PROTECTION CHECK — Run This Before Any Dismissal

Before initiating any dismissal routine, HR must verify the following. A "YES" to any of these questions means **STOP and escalate to Legal before doing anything else**.

| Check | Question | If YES |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **P1** | Is the employee currently pregnant, or have they notified pregnancy in the last 4 months? | ⛔ LEGAL GATE — absolute dismissal prohibition (§ 17 MuSchG) |
| **P2** | Is the employee currently on parental leave (Elternzeit), or have they applied for it? | ⛔ LEGAL GATE — dismissal prohibition (§ 18 BEEG) |
| **P3** | Does the employee have a recognized severe disability (GdB ≥ 50) or a pending GdB application that the company is aware of? | ⛔ LEGAL GATE — Integrationsamt approval required (§ 168 SGB IX) |
| **P4** | Is the employee a member of the Works Council (Betriebsrat)? | ⛔ LEGAL GATE — extraordinary dismissal requires Works Council consent (§ 103 BetrVG); ordinary dismissal is generally prohibited during mandate |
| **P5** | Is the employee the company's Data Protection Officer (Datenschutzbeauftragter)? | ⛔ LEGAL GATE — special dismissal protection (§ 38 BDSG) |
| **P6** | Has the employee filed a grievance, whistleblower report, or discrimination complaint in the last 12 months? | ⚠️ CAUTION — discuss with Legal before proceeding to avoid retaliation claim |
| **P7** | Is the employee on a recognized period of sick leave or long-term illness? | ⚠️ CAUTION — BEM requirement may apply; review SCEN-13 |

## PLAYBOOK 1: Dismissal During Probation Period

**Scenario:** Employee is within their agreed probation period (maximum 6 months) and employer wishes to end the employment.

**Legal framework:** § 622 Abs. 3 BGB — 2 weeks notice during probation. KSchG does not apply. All other protections (pregnancy, Elternzeit, Works Council, AGG) still apply.

---

### Step 1 — Verify Probation Status

- [ ] Pull the employment contract
- [ ] Confirm the start date and the agreed probation period (max 6 months by law)
- [ ] Calculate: Is today's date within the probation period?
- [ ] If the probation period has expired: this playbook does not apply — use Playbook 2 or 3 instead

> **Example:** Employee started 12 January 2026. Probation = 6 months. Probation expires 11 July 2026. Any dismissal must be issued AND RECEIVED by the employee before 11 July 2026.

---

### Step 2 — Run the Protection Check

Complete the Protection Check table above.

- [ ] P1 — Pregnancy check
- [ ] P2 — Elternzeit check
- [ ] P3 — Severe disability check
- [ ] P4 — Works Council membership check
- [ ] P5 — DPO check
- [ ] P6 — Grievance / whistleblower check

⛔ **If any check is positive: STOP — escalate to Legal.**

---

### Step 3 — Prepare the Works Council Consultation Package

Even during probation, Works Council consultation is mandatory (§ 102 BetrVG). The dismissal is void without it.

- [ ] Complete the Works Council Consultation Form (Template WC-1 below)
- [ ] Include: employee name, date of employment, position, dismissal type (ordinary, during probation), notice period (14 days), reason for dismissal
- [ ] Deliver to the Works Council in writing — email is acceptable for starting the window, but confirm receipt

> **Window:** Works Council has **7 calendar days** to respond. The dismissal letter cannot be issued before this window expires (or before the Works Council formally waives their response period).

---

### Step 4 — Draft the Dismissal Letter

Use Template DL-1 below. Key requirements:

- [ ] Written form — handwritten or typed and signed in wet ink (§ 623 BGB). Email is legally void.
- [ ] States that the employment is terminated during the probationary period
- [ ] States the correct notice period (14 days, or end of a calendar day if the contract specifies)
- [ ] States the termination date (notice period end date)
- [ ] Signed by an authorized person (HR Director, CEO, or authorized proxy — check signatory authority)

---

### Step 5 — Deliver the Letter

- [ ] **Preferred:** Deliver in person and obtain signed acknowledgment of receipt
- [ ] **Alternative:** Send by registered post with return receipt (Einschreiben mit Rückschein) — the date of attempted delivery counts, not the date the employee collects from the post office
- [ ] **Never:** Email, WhatsApp, or verbal notice — legally invalid

---

### Step 6 — Document Everything

- [ ] Copy of the Works Council consultation notice
- [ ] Works Council response (or record of non-response after 7 days)
- [ ] Copy of the signed dismissal letter
- [ ] Proof of delivery (acknowledgment signature or registered post receipt)
- [ ] File all documents in the employee's personnel file

---

### NOTICE PERIOD CALCULATOR (PROBATION)

- Notice period: **14 calendar days**
- Example: Letter delivered on **Monday 9 March 2026**
- Notice period ends: **Sunday 22 March 2026**
- Last day of employment: **Sunday 22 March 2026** (or the next working day if the contract refers to business days — check the contract)

## PLAYBOOK 2: Conduct Dismissal (After Probation, KSchG Applies)

**Scenario:** Employee (employed > 6 months, company > 10 FTE) is to be dismissed for conduct reasons. A prior written warning (Abmahnung) is required in most cases.

⚠️ **Litigation risk: HIGH. Legal review recommended before issuing.**

---

### Step 1 — Confirm KSchG Applies

- [ ] Employee tenure > 6 months? YES / NO
- [ ] Company has > 10 full-time equivalent employees? YES / NO

If both YES: KSchG applies. If NO to either: probation playbook applies or special rules — consult Legal.

---

### Step 2 — Run the Protection Check

Complete the Protection Check table (page 1).

⛔ **If any check is positive: STOP — escalate to Legal.**

---

### Step 3 — Verify the Prior Warning (Abmahnung)

- [ ] Locate the written Abmahnung in the employee's personnel file
- [ ] Confirm the Abmahnung relates to the same type of conduct as the current dismissal reason
- [ ] Confirm the Abmahnung was issued within the last 2–3 years (older warnings lose their evidentiary weight)
- [ ] Confirm the Abmahnung was issued after the conduct it refers to, not before

> **If no prior Abmahnung exists:** Unless the conduct is so serious that no reasonable employee could expect to keep their job (e.g., theft, fraud, physical assault), do not proceed to dismissal — issue an Abmahnung first. Use Playbook 5 (Abmahnung). Return to this playbook only if the conduct recurs.

⛔ **LEGAL GATE:** If the conduct is serious enough to consider skipping the Abmahnung — consult Legal first.

---

### Step 4 — Prepare the Works Council Consultation Package

- [ ] Complete Template WC-1
- [ ] Include: employee name, tenure, position, type of dismissal (ordinary, conduct-based), intended notice period, all incidents you are relying on (with dates), copies of any prior Abmahnungen
- [ ] **Critical:** Only incidents included in the Works Council package can be relied upon in court. Do not omit relevant incidents.
- [ ] Deliver to Works Council in writing

> **Window:** Works Council has **7 calendar days** to respond.

---

### Step 5 — Legal Review

⛔ **LEGAL GATE:** Share the consultation package and draft dismissal letter with Legal before issuing. Conduct dismissals under KSchG are litigated frequently and the legal standard is demanding.

---

### Step 6 — Draft and Deliver the Dismissal Letter

After Legal sign-off and Works Council window expiry:

- [ ] Draft dismissal letter using Template DL-2
- [ ] Confirm the correct notice period (see notice period table in legal-escalation-and-compliance.md)
- [ ] Obtain wet-ink signature from authorized signatory
- [ ] Deliver in person or by registered post
- [ ] Document delivery

---

## PLAYBOOK 3: Issuing a Written Warning (Abmahnung)

**Scenario:** HR wishes to formally warn an employee for conduct or repeated performance issues.

---

### Step 1 — Identify the Conduct to Warn About

- [ ] What exactly happened? (specific date, time, location, witnesses)
- [ ] Which contractual or policy obligation did it breach?
- [ ] Is the incident sufficiently recent? (incidents more than 2–3 years old may not support a warning)

---

### Step 2 — Check: Should This Be an Abmahnung or an Immediate Dismissal?

- [ ] Is the conduct serious enough to consider extraordinary dismissal (theft, fraud, assault)? If YES: escalate to Legal — do not issue an Abmahnung
- [ ] Has the employee received 2 or more prior Abmahnungen for similar conduct? If YES: consider whether dismissal is now appropriate — escalate to Legal

---

### Step 3 — Check: Works Council Member?

- [ ] Is the employee a Works Council member?
- [ ] If YES: Is the conduct related to their Works Council activities? If YES: ⛔ STOP — an Abmahnung for Works Council activities is prohibited

---

### Step 4 — Draft the Abmahnung

Use Template AB-1 below. The Abmahnung must contain:

1. **Specific conduct description** — what happened, when, where, who witnessed it
2. **Statement of breach** — which contractual or policy obligation was violated (be specific)
3. **Instruction to desist** — clear direction not to repeat the conduct
4. **Warning of consequence** — explicit statement that repetition may result in dismissal without further warning
5. **Date and authorized signature**

> ⚠️ **Do not use vague language.** "Your attitude needs to improve" is not an Abmahnung. "On 5 March 2026 you raised your voice at a colleague in the open-plan office in front of six witnesses, in breach of the company Code of Conduct §3.2" is an Abmahnung.

---

### Step 5 — Issue and Document

- [ ] Deliver the Abmahnung to the employee in writing — in person with signed acknowledgment of receipt, or by registered post
- [ ] Inform the employee they have the right to respond in writing and have their response attached to their personnel file
- [ ] File the signed Abmahnung (and any employee response) in the personnel file
- [ ] Note in calendar: if no repeat conduct within 24 months, the Abmahnung's effectiveness weakens — reassess

## PLAYBOOK 4: Works Council Consultation (§ 102 BetrVG)

**Scenario:** Any dismissal is being planned. Works Council consultation must be completed before the dismissal letter is issued.

---

### When Is This Required?

Works Council consultation under § 102 BetrVG is required before **every dismissal** — ordinary, extraordinary, and during probation — provided a Works Council exists at the company. Check: does TechForward currently have a Works Council?

---

### Consultation Window

| Dismissal Type | Works Council Response Window |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| Ordinary dismissal | **7 calendar days** |
| Extraordinary (immediate) dismissal | **3 calendar days** |

The window runs from the date the Works Council **receives** the complete consultation package — not from the date HR sent it.

---

### Step 1 — Prepare the Consultation Package (Template WC-1)

The package must include all of the following. Incomplete packages reset the clock when corrected information is provided.

- [ ] Employee's full name and date of birth
- [ ] Employee's position and department
- [ ] Date employment began
- [ ] Type of dismissal (ordinary / extraordinary / redundancy)
- [ ] The intended notice period and termination date (for ordinary dismissal)
- [ ] The reason for dismissal — full facts, including all incidents being relied upon
- [ ] For conduct dismissal: copies of prior Abmahnungen
- [ ] For redundancy: the Sozialauswahl calculation and result
- [ ] Whether the employee is subject to any special protection, and how it has been addressed

---

### Step 2 — Deliver to the Works Council

- [ ] Deliver in writing — email with read receipt is acceptable
- [ ] Note the date and time of delivery — this starts the clock
- [ ] Retain the delivery confirmation

---

### Step 3 — Wait for the Response Window

- [ ] Set a reminder for the expiry of the window (7 days / 3 days from delivery)
- [ ] The dismissal letter **cannot** be issued before the window expires
- [ ] If the Works Council raises a formal objection (Widerspruch) for an ordinary dismissal, the dismissal can still proceed but the employee gains the right to continued employment pending any court challenge

---

### Step 4 — After the Window

- [ ] Document: Works Council response (or non-response) with date
- [ ] Proceed to issue the dismissal letter
- [ ] If the Works Council raised objections: attach a copy of their objection to the dismissal letter (§ 102 Abs. 4 BetrVG)

---

## PLAYBOOK 5: Fixed-Term Contract (Sachgrundlose Befristung)

**Scenario:** HR wants to hire an employee on a fixed-term contract without a specific objective reason.

---

### Step 1 — Check Prior Employment

⚠️ **Critical check:** Has this person ever worked for TechForward before, in any capacity (including internships, freelance, or temporary work)?

- [ ] Search the HR system by full name and date of birth
- [ ] If YES: sachgrundlose Befristung is **prohibited** — the contract will be treated as permanent from day one. Consider Sachgrundbefristung (with objective reason) instead or hire permanently.

---

### Step 2 — Check Duration and Extension Limits

- [ ] Is this a new contract or an extension?
- [ ] Calculate total fixed-term duration including this contract/extension — must not exceed **24 months**
- [ ] Count total number of extensions — must not exceed **3**

---

### Step 3 — Draft the Contract

- [ ] Use the standard fixed-term contract template
- [ ] Both parties must sign **before** the employment start date (§ 14 Abs. 4 TzBfG)
- [ ] The written contract must be in place before work begins — a verbal agreement does not create a valid Befristung

---

### Step 4 — Works Council Notification (Companies ≥ 20 employees)

- [ ] Submit a § 99 BetrVG notification to the Works Council before the employee starts
- [ ] Allow the Works Council 1 week to respond — they can raise objections

---

### Step 5 — Set Renewal Review Reminder

- [ ] Set a calendar reminder 3 months before the contract end date
- [ ] Decision required: extend (within limits), convert to permanent, or allow to lapse
- [ ] If extending: new signed extension agreement must be in place **before** the current term expires — even one day late converts the contract to permanent

[NAME]

TechForward Solutions GmbH
