# TechForward HR — Legal Escalation Rules & Compliance Reference

## Purpose

This document provides HR with:
1. **Statutory notice period table** — correct notice periods by tenure under § 622 BGB
2. **Protected employee categories** — who cannot be dismissed without special process
3. **Hard legal deadlines** — time limits that cannot be extended
4. **HR authority matrix** — what HR can decide alone vs. what requires Legal
5. **Prohibited actions** — things HR must never do

---

## 1. STATUTORY NOTICE PERIODS (§ 622 BGB)

These are the minimum statutory notice periods for employer-initiated ordinary dismissal. Check the employment contract and any applicable collective agreement (Tarifvertrag) — these may provide longer periods.

| Length of Service (completed years) | Minimum Notice Period | To Which Date |
|--------------------------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| During probation (max 6 months) | **14 calendar days** | Any day |
| After probation, up to 2 years | **4 weeks** | 15th or last day of calendar month |
| 2 years | **1 month** | End of calendar month |
| 5 years | **2 months** | End of calendar month |
| 8 years | **3 months** | End of calendar month |
| 10 years | **4 months** | End of calendar month |
| 12 years | **5 months** | End of calendar month |
| 15 years | **6 months** | End of calendar month |
| 20 years | **7 months** | End of calendar month |

> **How to calculate the termination date:**
> 1. Determine the correct notice period based on tenure (years of completed service at the time the notice is issued)
> 2. Count forward from the delivery date of the dismissal letter
> 3. The notice period ends on the 15th or last day of the calendar month (except during probation, where any calendar day applies)
>
> **Example:** Employee has 11 years of service. Notice issued 5 March 2026. Notice period = 5 months to end of month. Count 5 months forward from 5 March: 5 April, 5 May, 5 June, 5 July, 5 August. End of that month = **31 August 2026**.

> **Important:** Employee-initiated notice periods are shorter — employees can generally give 4 weeks' notice to the 15th or end of the month. The employer cannot use the employee's shorter period; the table above applies only to employer-initiated dismissal.

## 2. PROTECTED EMPLOYEE CATEGORIES

These employees cannot be dismissed through normal HR processes. Special approval or legal process is required. **The protection check must be completed before any dismissal action.**

### Category 1 — Absolute Protection: Approval Required from State Authority

| Category | Legal Basis | Protection Period | State Authority |
|----------|-------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Pregnant employees** | § 17 MuSchG | From start of pregnancy to 4 months after birth | Aufsichtsbehörde (Gewerbeaufsicht / Landesamt für Arbeitsschutz) |
| **Employees on parental leave (Elternzeit)** | § 18 BEEG | From date of Elternzeit application to end of Elternzeit | Zuständige Behörde (varies by state) |

> **Key rule:** The protection applies even if the employer does not yet know about the pregnancy or Elternzeit application — provided the employee notifies within 2 weeks of receiving a dismissal letter. A dismissal that has already been issued is retroactively void if the employee notifies pregnancy within 2 weeks.

---

### Category 2 — Procedural Protection: Integrationsamt Approval Required

| Category | Legal Basis | Protection | Process Duration |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Severely disabled employees (GdB ≥ 50)** | §§ 168–175 SGB IX | Integrationsamt (Inklusionsamt) approval required before any dismissal | 4–6 weeks |
| **Employees with equal status (GdB 30–49 with Gleichstellung)** | § 151 SGB IX | Same as above | 4–6 weeks |

> **GdB application pending:** Protection applies if the employer is aware that an employee has applied for a disability determination, even before the GdB is formally recognized.

---

### Category 3 — Procedural Protection: Works Council Consent or Court Authorization Required

| Category | Legal Basis | Protection | Process |
|----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| **Works Council members** | § 15 KSchG, § 103 BetrVG | Ordinary dismissal prohibited during mandate and for 1 year after. Extraordinary dismissal requires Works Council consent (§ 103 BetrVG) or labor court authorization. | Application to Works Council or Arbeitsgericht |
| **Youth and Apprentice Representative (JAV)** | § 15 KSchG | Same as Works Council members | Same |
| **Spokesperson Committee members (Sprecherausschuss)** | SprAuG | Similar to Works Council members | Similar |

---

### Category 4 — Special Protection: Enhanced Notice / Requirements

| Category | Legal Basis | Additional Requirement |
|----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Data Protection Officer (DSB/DPO)** | § 38 Abs. 2 BDSG, § 6 Abs. 4 DSGVO | Cannot be dismissed or disadvantaged for performing their role. Ordinary dismissal possible for other reasons but enhanced scrutiny. 1-year post-mandate protection. |
| **Whistleblowers / complainants** | AGG § 16, HinSchG | Must not be dismissed or disadvantaged as retaliation for a protected complaint. Burden of proof shifts to employer under AGG § 22. |
| **Employees on sick leave** | § 1 KSchG, § 167 SGB IX | BEM must be offered for employees with >6 weeks sick leave in 12 months before illness-related dismissal. |

## 3. HARD LEGAL DEADLINES

These deadlines are set by law and cannot be extended. Missing them has severe consequences.

| Deadline | Law | Period | Consequence of Missing |
|----------|-----|--------|----------------------|
| **Extraordinary dismissal must be issued** | § 626 Abs. 2 BGB | Within **2 weeks** of the authorized decision-maker becoming aware of the dismissal reason | The extraordinary dismissal is void — only ordinary dismissal remains as an option (with long notice period) |
| **Works Council consultation — ordinary dismissal** | § 102 BetrVG | Works Council has **7 calendar days** to respond | Dismissal cannot be issued before window expires. Premature dismissal is void. |
| **Works Council consultation — extraordinary dismissal** | § 102 BetrVG | Works Council has **3 calendar days** to respond | Same — dismissal cannot be issued before window expires |
| **Employee challenge to dismissal at Arbeitsgericht** | § 4 KSchG | Employee must file within **3 weeks** of receiving the dismissal letter | After 3 weeks, the dismissal is legally deemed valid even if it was improper |
| **Fixed-term contract extension must be signed** | § 14 TzBfG | Before the current contract term expires | If extension is signed after expiry, the contract becomes a permanent employment contract immediately |
| **Employer response to part-time request (TzBfG)** | § 8 TzBfG | Within **1 month** of the employee's request | Silence = approval. The employee's requested arrangement takes effect automatically. |
| **Employer response to Elternzeit part-time request** | § 15 BEEG | Within **4 weeks** of the request | Silence = approval |
| **AGG discrimination complaint by rejected candidate** | § 15 Abs. 4 AGG | Candidate must claim compensation within **2 months** of rejection | After 2 months, claim is time-barred — retain recruitment documentation for 2 months minimum |
| **GDPR data subject request (employee)** | DSGVO Art. 12 | Respond within **1 calendar month** | Regulatory fine up to EUR 20M / 4% global turnover |

## 4. WORKS COUNCIL RIGHTS BY SITUATION

| Situation | Works Council Right | HR Action Required |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Any dismissal (ordinary or extraordinary) | **Anhörung** (§ 102 BetrVG) — must be heard before dismissal | Submit consultation package; wait for response window |
| New hire (companies ≥ 20 employees) | **Zustimmung** (§ 99 BetrVG) — consent required before employment begins | Submit notification; allow 1-week response |
| Working time changes | **Mitbestimmung** (§ 87 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BetrVG) — co-determination | Negotiate with Works Council |
| Introduction of technical monitoring (e.g., time tracking, activity monitoring software) | **Mitbestimmung** (§ 87 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 BetrVG) — co-determination | Works Council agreement required before implementation |
| Large-scale restructuring (≥ 20 employees affected) | **Interessenausgleich / Sozialplan** (§§ 111–113 BetrVG) — negotiation right | Notify Works Council early; negotiate Interessenausgleich |
| Dismissal of Works Council member | **Consent** (§ 103 BetrVG) — Works Council (without the affected member) must consent to extraordinary dismissal | Apply to Works Council or labor court |
| BEM process (sick employees) | **Involvement** (§ 167 SGB IX) — employee may involve Works Council | Inform employee of this right at start of BEM |

## 6. PROHIBITED ACTIONS

HR must **never** do the following without explicit Legal authorization:

1. **Issue any dismissal letter before the Works Council consultation window has expired** — the dismissal is automatically void
2. **Issue a dismissal letter by email, SMS, or any electronic means** — § 623 BGB requires written form with wet-ink signature; electronic communication is legally void
3. **Proceed with any dismissal of a pregnant employee, employee on Elternzeit, or severely disabled employee** without state authority / Integrationsamt approval
4. **Dismiss a Works Council member through ordinary dismissal** — this is legally impossible and attempting it may constitute a criminal offense under § 119 BetrVG
5. **Issue an extraordinary dismissal more than 2 weeks after the decision-maker first became aware of the reason** — the 2-week deadline is absolute
6. **Sign a fixed-term extension agreement after the current contract has expired** — doing so converts the employment to permanent with retroactive effect
7. **Make any statement to a departing employee that constitutes an admission of liability** without Legal sign-off
8. **Conduct a redundancy dismissal without a documented Sozialauswahl** — the dismissal will be overturned
9. **Ask interview candidates about pregnancy, family plans, disability, religion, or other AGG-protected characteristics** — this creates liability under AGG § 15
10. **Retain recruitment documentation for less than 2 months after rejection** — needed to defend against AGG claims
11. **Issue an Abmahnung to a Works Council member for activities related to their Works Council role** — this is prohibited conduct
12. **Proceed with any employment action based on assumptions about a protected characteristic** (e.g., assuming an employee is not pregnant because they haven't said so)
