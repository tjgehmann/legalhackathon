The Hidden Penalties

Gemini in Google Docs
▼

Your Mission

Your legal team just received a 19-section draft consulting agreement — 8 pages of dense legal text. Before signing, you need to find every hidden penalty buried in the fine print. The penalties use tricky language: "surcharge", "deduction", "levy", "remediation fee"... A simple Ctrl+F won't catch them all. But AI understands what a penalty means.

  1. Open the shared Google Doc called "Station 1 — The Hidden Penalties"
  2. Open the Gemini sidebar (click the Gemini star icon on the right, or go to Tools > Gemini)
  3. Ask Gemini: Find all clauses in this contract that reference a Penalty Schedule. For each one, tell me the section number, penalty amount, reason and the Penalty Schedule letter.
  4. List the Penalty Schedule letters in the order they appear — that spells your code word
There are exactly 6 Penalty Schedules hidden across the contract. Each has a single letter (like "Penalty Schedule B"). Read those 6 letters in section order.

The Contract Portfolio Review

Gemini in Google Sheets
▼

Your Mission

You're the legal ops manager. Your contract portfolio has 50 active contracts — and some are about to silently auto-renew without proper compliance certification. You need to find them before it's too late. Today's date is March 29, 2026.

  1. Open the shared Google Sheet called "Station 2 — Contract Portfolio Tracker"
  2. Open the Gemini sidebar
  3. Ask Gemini: Today's date is March 29, 2026. Which contracts expire within the next 3 months (before July 1, 2026), have auto-renewal enabled, AND have an expired compliance certification? These are contracts that will silently auto-renew without proper compliance. Sort results by expiry date, earliest first. Create a short table here in the chat.
  4. Gemini will return 5 contracts. Take the first letter of each counterparty name and read them together — that's your code word
There are exactly 5 contracts matching all 3 criteria. Watch out for near-misses: some are expired but not auto-renewing, some auto-renew but have valid certification. When sorted by expiry date, the first letters spell a 5-letter word meaning "to build."

The Scrambled Brief

Gemini Chat (gemini.google.com)
▼

Your Mission

A frantic colleague sent this chaotic email. Your job: use AI to turn chaos into clarity.

"hi so basically the client called and theyre upset about the contract thing from last week where the delivery dates got mixed up and also they think the penalty clause is too harsh and they want to renegotiate but our sales team already promised them different terms verbally which we never put in writing and now legal needs to sort this out before thursday or the client says theyll walk and also procurement wants to know if we can just extend the existing framework agreement instead of doing a whole new contract which would save time but im not sure if thats compliant with our policies can someone please advise asap thanks"
  1. Go to gemini.google.com
  2. Paste the email text above and ask Gemini: Rewrite this as a structured legal memo to the Legal Department with clear, numbered action items
  3. Count the number of distinct action items Gemini identifies
  4. Your code word is that number, written as a word (e.g., "three", "seven")
Most teams get a number between 4 and 6. Read carefully — some action items might be combined. The most common answer is a number right in the middle of that range.

The Negotiation Thread

Gemini in Google Docs
▼

Your Mission

You're in-house counsel. A 15-email negotiation thread just landed on your desk — 10 people from sales, legal, finance, IT, and the vendor have been going back and forth for weeks. Before the contract gets signed, you need to know: who already made a binding commitment on behalf of the company? Promises, agreed terms, authorized access — you need to find them all.

  1. Open the shared Google Doc called "Station 4 — Vendor Negotiation Thread"
  2. Open the Gemini sidebar
  3. Ask Gemini: Which people in this email thread made a binding commitment or promise on behalf of TechCorp? A binding commitment means they confirmed a specific timeline, agreed to contract terms, authorized access to systems, or locked in financial conditions — not just providing information, asking questions, or forwarding emails. List only the people who made commitments, in chronological order.
  4. Gemini will identify 5 people. Take the first letter of each person's FIRST NAME (in chronological order) and read them together — that's your code word
10 people are in the thread, but only 5 actually committed to something. The others just asked questions, forwarded emails, or reported findings. Focus on who said "I've confirmed", "I've agreed", "I've authorized", or "I've committed." Their first names' first letters spell a 5-letter word.
